# Session Recap: Dressed to Kill

*Party: Old Man Tarnish, Dhallox Plat, Torvin, and Larn — GM'd by Eric*

Night had settled over Luthadel's merchant district, and the crew had a single thread to pull: Velstane's tailor shop, and whatever it knew about their missing leader, Enge. The shop was dark, the windows showed nothing but dresses and headless mannequins, and a perception check turned up no sign of a scuffle outside. The only odd detail was on the door itself: a sign that said CLOSED, hanging on a door that was very slightly open.

## Window Shopping After Dark

Larn put his finger to his lips and urged everyone to keep it quiet. Old Man Tarnish took the opposite approach and shuffled in first with a deliberate limp, hoping to look as harmless as possible. The showroom was all polished mirrors, dark wood, and rows of mannequins in evening wear. Beyond it lay a velvet-draped fitting area, and Tarnish dug through the couch cushions and came up with two boxings, which he guarded with the devotion of a man who has never once been rich. Out in the inner courtyard, Dhallox cheated by burning metal and homing in on the only shiny thing there: a silvery ribbon some bird had carried off.

## Alterations Required

The workshop was a tangle of half-finished gowns and masquerade masks threaded with gold. Dhallox found the tailor's ledger, full of custom orders for Houses Helarial, Tekiel, and Buvidas, with one name underlined again and again: Guiverre Seeris. Beneath it sat a stack of urgent commissions for masks, dyed gloves, and cloaks, and a note signed "V": *ensure the slit in the dress is wide enough; she may need to access a blade.* Every item had already been delivered. Tarnish committed it all to his coppermind, which meant he promptly forgot it and had to rely on everyone else's notes.

The best find came from the torn-curtain fitting room. Larn found a fingerless knit glove that was unmistakably Enge's and tucked it into his satchel. Behind a tall mirror, the wall swung outward to reveal a staircase leading down into a flickering glow. "Glad we didn't leave," Dhallox said, "we almost tried knocking down the door of a noble's house."

## Two Guards and a Portcullis

At the bottom of the stairs, two startled guards scrambled for their weapons and told the party they had no business being down there. Larn tried to talk them down ("Velstane sent us!"), but diplomacy failed against men already reaching for steel. The fight was quick and not especially graceful, though there was one very loud portcullis involved. The gate slammed shut and sprang open again, to the group's amusement, as the room split in two. Larn charged in with his glass dagger. Dhallox laid into the nearest guard with his dueling cane until the man was seeing double. A single blow from the other guard, a man on what he'd announced was his last day before retirement, left Dhallox clinging to one hit point. Tarnish hauled the gate high enough for Torvin to slip through, and then tried to look very cool while missing a flourishing back-swing entirely. Larn knocked one guard out cold with the flat of his dagger, and Dhallox pressed his zinc to the other, in a very polite way, until he gave up and dozed off.

The loot was an old side sword, a longbow, and a peanut butter and jelly sandwich wrapped in a note from the guard's family wishing him a happy last day. The note had a granddaughter's drawing on it. Larn took it, announcing it would go on the wall back on the boat.

## Busts, Boxes, and Bad Ideas

At the base of the stairs, a branching hallway of stone busts glared down at them. Dhallox, the only former noble in the group, named them one by one: Sean Helarial, Delina Tekiel, Idrin Sirus, and a roll call of Urbanes, Buvidas, and others, all heirs of the great houses. He also remembered that Delina had once publicly disinvited him from her gala, which stung. Sean he knew to be dead. Dhallox spent the rest of the inspection drawing a mustache on one of the busts.

The first door opened into a storeroom of tidy crates holding dozens of inert spikes, some pristine and some caked in blood. Torvin, a former Steel Ministry man, knew exactly what he was looking at and didn't enjoy it. Larn found a crate of surgical tools and weak antiseptic (five doses, good for a little healing after a rest). The empty crates reeked faintly of sulfur and held a smear of blood and a few strands of hair too crusted to color-match to Enge. The party left with twenty-four spikes, and an unwelcome idea about what they were for.

## Where the Deed Was Done

The next room was worse. Steel tables, chains, empty cells, and a cell door torn off its hinges. An old Obligator sat on the floor with a flask, staring at nothing. Torvin, who still wore the mask Enge had made to hide his tattoos, took it off for him, and the old man smiled at the sight of a fellow lost soul. Dhallox inflamed his fear, and Larn pressed. The Obligator told them the room had been a laboratory built to create something like a Steel Inquisitor, though in the service of a plan to make a god. The mastermind was a man named Vadelek, and his masterwork was the *eternity spike*. Enge, he said, had refused it, and Vadelek had dragged her to the crucible across the hall. But there was a problem there: the Obligator's latest failed experiment, enraged by the noise, had broken out into the corridor. Torvin hauled the old man up and dragged him along for the rest of the night. He offered no resistance, as if this were his penance.

## The Beast in the Crucible

Larn threw open the door, and the corridor's occupant burst through it: a hulking, transparent-skinned brute with taut red muscle, black eyes, a steel bar in its hands, and a thin silver spike jutting from its neck. Larn saw a floor of bodies beyond it and was torn between rushing to help them and fighting what stood in front of him, so he did both, badly. The beast was a boss, so it got two turns a round and spent them on Larn and Tarnish. Dhallox took two actions to catch his breath, then gave Torvin a leadership boost. Tarnish tried to snatch the spike and missed, then spent focus on wild swings that missed even more. Torvin lined up, pulled the spike from its neck, and the monster screamed. It had just lost the power that would have made it nearly unbeatable.

It fled into the crucible, and the party followed. Hanging over the room was a rusted chandelier. Torvin threw a glass dagger at the chain, which snapped, and the chandelier landed on the beast. It didn't get up.

## What the Skaa Saw

The crucible held two steel tables, a locked cell of terrified skaa, and a dozen dead of the same. On one table lay a Steel Inquisitor with spikes through his eyes and a collapsed torso, and on the other lay a lot of dried blood. A barefoot skaa teenager unlocked the cell at the party's urging and stared at them. When the teen saw the old Obligator, he said to watch out for that one: a real sicko. The teen's account was chaotic. A bunch of young nobles in skull masks had arrived, the Inquisitor had given a speech about sacrifice and stabbed himself through the chest, and a scarred young woman had taken his axe and told the others that to be the heir they would have to kill her. Then a brawl of Allomancy had broken out, and the redhead on the table had been hurt badly before vanishing. In exchange for passage to Luthadel, the teen told them where the survivors had gone (north), and Larn shook on it, then immediately wiped his hand on the teen's clothes.

Larn checked the bodies with a medic's eye. A young noblewoman had willingly taken a hole through the heart, with no signs of a struggle. The dead skaa showed bruises and torn fingernails. There were coins embedded in the walls, dents in the tables, and the bloody footprints of at least nine people leading out the north door. Torvin read the Inquisitor's body: several spikes had been removed, and the killing blow had been his own.

## The Library of a Dead Man

Tarnish went to the library, and Torvin followed to help with a safe that wouldn't yield to brute strength. A handbag on the table held twenty-five boxings and an invitation: *the ascension ceremony begins at nightfall; only the worthy will endure.* In the study next door hung a portrait of Vadelek, a bald, tattooed Obligator, standing beside a young auburn-haired woman with bold eyebrows. It was Enge. Beneath it, a bronze chest gave way to Tarnish's nimble fingers and revealed Vadelek's journal. It held training logs ranking his favorite young nobles (the Gilded Few) against each other, with Sean Helarial's name crossed out. The scores of Delina Tekiel surpassed the rest. There was a sketch of the whole party circled around Enge, and notes on the back tracking their movements between Luthadel and Urteau, written in a different hand from Vadelek's. Someone had been watching them for months.

The last page had been torn out, but the journal's cover read 16-50-36, which opened the safe. Inside were two leather-bound tomes in cipher, and as it turned out, full of hemalurgic information. A torn page from the Inquisitor's pocket read like a last will: Vadelek's sacrifice would catalyze the future, and should he falter, contingencies would ensure a worthy heir. Tarnish also pocketed a bottle of archival ink laced with metal particles, which doesn't fade with age. Anything the party writes with it, Eric noted, may well resurface in future eras.

## A Welcome Sight

Larn followed the footprints north, opening the last door. Behind it was a figure under a mask of dried blood, who looked up with fear turning to relief. "Lord Ruler," Enge said, with a sobbing breath. "You are a welcome sight." Larn hugged her, checked her wounds (she'd been spiked, but was standing), and listened. Her uncle Vadelek had invited her to a meeting with his secret mentorship group, and had wanted to make her a god. After he sacrificed himself, Delina Tekiel attacked her for being the chosen one, and in the chaos of the Allomantic brawl, she had used her skill to snatch the eternity spike and hide. She unwrapped her coat to show them: a large, silvery-gray spike. It made them a target, so the safest place to take it was Luthadel, among friends, away from the nobility's reach.

The party and Enge left with five skaa in tow, and Torvin's captive Obligator stayed behind.

## Cold Storage for Nobles

The lift carried them up into a quiet warehouse of fabric bolts and crates. Along the north wall lay two six-foot wooden boxes, one ajar. Inside was Idrin Sirus, preserved and dressed in finery, with a letter from his father clutched in his fingers: *you do not need to ascend to Vadelek's whims or his damn system; come home.* The other coffin held Anite Urbane. Both had been sacrificed to build a lattice of spikes, their powers stolen. The warehouse belonged to Velstane's shop, and the party stepped out of the door to find themselves across the street from where they'd started the night.

## Cliffhanger

The party has the eternity spike, a trail of bloody footprints from nine fleeing nobles, two cipher tomes, a bottle of ink that outlives everything, and a sketch proving someone had been watching them for months. They also have a promise to five skaa, one of whom now knows a great deal about Larn's hand-washing habits, and a guilty conscience over a monster who'll never be identified. The next session picks up from here, with a trip to Luthadel on the cards, and Veterans Day (the 11th) is the date.
